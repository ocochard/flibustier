# The flags of the open pack

The game shows a ship's colours as a flag waving on its staff.
`flag.py` waves each flag and `assets.toml` describes its design.

The game has two kinds of flag:
- four nations' flags (`pic/f-*`);
- twenty Jolly Rogers (`pic/fjolly01` to `20`), one for each named
  pirate captain.

This file gathers what is known of the real flags, from the research of
2026-10-28. Each claim is marked:
- **[C]** a period source describes it;
- **[M]** a modern attribution or invention;
- **[?]** no attribution found.

Many famous "pirate flags" are 20th-century inventions. The pack keeps
the game's designs, so a player still knows each captain by his flag,
but draws them as a period flag would look.

## How a pirate flag looked

- **Cloth.** Wool bunting, silk or linen.
  - Roberts flew "a black Silk Flag" (GH).
  - Dulaien's flag of 1729 was a fine cloth 22 ft 9 in by 14 ft 9 in,
    about 3 by 2: a full ensign for a large ship.
- **The figures.** Crews often made their flags aboard: "a black
  Ensign was made" (GH, Spriggs). The figures were painted, as on the
  drawing of Dulaien's flag, or sewn on. Either way they are flat, crude
  and of one colour: white, or sometimes red. They are never shaded or
  drawn like a picture.
- **Colours.**
  - Black is the commonest.
  - Red is the "bloody flag", meaning no quarter: "black and bloody
    Flags hanging over us" (GH, Macrae).
  - Red figures appear on black: "a black Flag, with the Figure of Death
    in red" (GH, Low).
  - Plain black or red flags with no figure were common in the 17th
    century.
- **Dates.** The skull and crossbones appears at sea in 1688, on French
  corsairs' red flag. Pirates used it regularly from about 1700, and the
  designs spread from about 1715.
- **Wear.** Sun-faded, frayed and torn at the fly, stained by powder.
- **Survivals.** No pirate flag of the Golden Age survives.

The pack therefore draws a Jolly Roger as worn wool bunting, its figures
in flat white (or red) paint or appliqué, crude, unshaded, faded and
torn at the fly.

## The game's Jolly Rogers

These are the designs as the game draws them, with what is known of
each.

1. **A skeleton with an hourglass and a spear, facing a man raising a
   cup** (Roberts). The toasting man is **[M]**. At Whydah GH gives "a
   Death in it, with an Hour-Glass in one Hand, and cross Bones in the
   other, a Dart by it". Eyewitnesses saw "a Black Flag with Death's
   head and a cutlass" (1720) and "a white skeleton" (1722) **[C]**.
2. **A man standing on two skulls lettered ABH and AMH** (Roberts):
   "a Man pourtray'd in it, with a flaming Sword in his Hand, and
   standing on two Skulls, subscribed A B H and A M H" **[C, GH only]**.
   The skulls stand for a Barbadian's head and a Martinican's.
3. **Three skulls, each over crossed bones.** This is **[M]**: first
   printed in 1912 and tied to Condent only in 1933. Condent's own flags
   had "a Deaths Head" **[C]**.
4. **A skull over crossed bones** (England, also Taylor and Bellamy):
   "a large black flag, with a Death's Head and Bones across" **[C]**.
5. **A skull above crossed bones.** Worley flew "a black Ensign, with a
   white Death's Head in the Middle of it" **[C]**.
6. **A skull over crossed bones, with an hourglass** (Emanuel Wynne,
   1700): "a sable ensigne with Cross bones, a Death's head, and an hour
   glase" **[C]**. It is the earliest attributed pirate skull and
   crossbones.
7. **A skull in a headscarf over crossed bones** (Every). **[M]**,
   invented for a book of 1959; there is no evidence Every flew any
   such flag.
8. **A skull above crossed cutlasses** (Rackham). **[M]**, tied to
   Rackham in 1959 and perhaps from the film *Captain Blood* (1935).
   Witnesses at his trial mention only "a white pendant".
9. **A large skull over one long bone.** **[?]**
10. **A skull over a bone and a sword.** **[?]** The nearest period
    flag is Roberts' "Death's head and a cutlass" **[C]**.
11. **A skull in a hat, over a bone and a sword.** **[?]**
12. **A skull and bone, a dagger and a red heart** (Bonnet). **[M]**.
    His contemporaries describe "a Death's Head" and a "bloody flag"
    **[C]**.
13. **An arm holding a cutlass** (Tew). **[M]**, a 20th-century
    attribution. An arm with a cutlass is genuine on Roberts' flag of
    1720 at Trepassey **[C]**.
14. **A man with a cutlass, and a small skull and crossbones.** The
    nearest period flag is Dulaien's (1729) **[C]**: "white designs of
    human figures, cutlasses, bones, and hourglasses", with a naked man
    holding an hourglass and a sabre.
15. **A horned skeleton with a spear at a bleeding heart, and an
    hourglass** (Blackbeard). **[M]**: first drawn in 1911, the horns
    added later.
    - The unhorned "Old Roger" is Low's and Spriggs': "a white
      Skeliton in the Middle of it, with a Dart in one Hand striking a
      bleeding Heart, and in the other, an Hour-Glass" (GH) **[C]**.
    - Blackbeard flew a black flag with a death's head **[C]**.
16. **A skull and a red heart.** **[?]**
17. **A skull over crossed bones, a heart above.** **[?]**
18. **A red skeleton on black** (Low): "a black Flag, with the Figure
    of Death in red" **[C]**.
19. **A red flag with an arm and a sword and a golden skull** (Moody).
    **[M]**: tied to Moody in 1933, and Moody was never a captain. A red
    "bloody flag" itself is **[C]**.
20. **A skull with flaming hair over a cutlass.** **[?]**

## The nations' flags at sea, about 1650-1700

The game draws its own national flags: Spain's royal arms, England's
cross, France's fleurs-de-lis and Holland's lion. The flags really
flown at sea were these:

- **Spain**: the Cross of Burgundy, a ragged red saltire on white. It
  was the flag of merchants and privateers until 1785, and flew over
  the New World's forts. The naval ensign was white with the royal arms
  after 1700. The red-yellow-red flag only came in 1785.
- **England**: the red ensign, red with a St George's cross in the
  canton, made the merchant flag in 1674; a plain St George's cross was
  also flown. The Union canton only came in 1707.
- **France**: a plain white flag for the royal navy, or white strewn
  with gold fleurs-de-lis. Merchants flew a white cross on blue. The
  tricolour only came after 1790.
- **Holland**: red-white-blue, which the navy flew always after 1663.
  The orange-white-blue Prince's flag belongs to before about 1660.

The pack keeps the game's four national flags, so a player knows them
as before. The real ones above would be a choice for the maintainer.
